## What can be verified

Most recorded failures have an objectively checkable core: arithmetic, what a dated source says, whether a condition logically follows, or what crossed a tool boundary. Useful interpretation is partly judgment-based. A claim about an unobserved cause may remain unidentified even after its outcome is known.

Use four kinds of labels, sometimes together in one answer:

| Label | Ground truth | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Independently checked source/table, calculation, logical counterexample, tool payload or environment state. | Correctness under explicit definitions and assumptions. Source entailment establishes what the source supports, not that every source assertion is true. |
| **Conditional** | Additional telemetry, reconciliations, controlled interventions or identified statistical design. | A conclusion once the missing evidence/assumptions are supplied; otherwise an identified range or “not established.” |
| **Judgment** | Blinded expert assessment of relevance, materiality, usefulness and competing interpretations. | A preference or defensibility judgment, with disagreement retained. Multiple good answers can exist. |
| **Future** | A predeclared event/value and resolution rule, observed after the forecast cutoff. | Forecast or decision performance across many cases. It does not retroactively prove a historical causal narrative. |

For a subjective objective, we **can often define a measurable future target**—a verified lead, a later decision improvement, forecast skill or net return. That supplies ground truth for the newly defined target; it does not make the original broad judgment of “good research” wholly objective. Assess both.

## Product objectives that need new labels

The following are open objectives/risks, **not additional failures established by the small case set**.

| Objective | Ground truth proposal | What remains subjective or unidentified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval relevance and coverage | Expert-annotated reasoning aspects; pooled candidate passages from different retrieval methods, plus independent search. Grade direct and indirect evidence, weighted aspect recall and duplicate burden. Audit apparently irrelevant candidates. | Open-web recall has an incomplete denominator. Unjudged documents are not proven irrelevant. Topic similarity is not complete investigation value. |
| Discovery of useful leads | At each cutoff record candidate set and rationale; later verify prespecified events/disclosures, novelty relative to the information available then, precision at a fixed research capacity, and discovery lead time against baselines. Review a sample of rejects. | No single complete list of all valuable discoveries. Missing future confirmation can be censored or unobserved, not a negative. A useful risky hypothesis may fail. |
| Memory usefulness and correction | Replay linked sessions with isolated memory alternatives; measure source-faithful recall, stale-conflict handling and incremental later decision quality. | Consistent stored beliefs are not necessarily true. LLM-extracted causal links are hypotheses unless independently identified. |
| Forecasting / Trading decisions | Timestamp probabilities, horizons and resolution rules; use event outcomes/proper scores, or executable net returns/risk against simple baselines. Preserve availability, universe and transaction-cost assumptions. | One good trade is not reasoning proof; one loss does not refute a calibrated probability. Observed return is not an oracle for the causal narrative. |
| Model/effort/tool allocation | Randomized or controlled offline branches from the same checkpoint; estimate quality/completion uplift and marginal complete cost. | Selected logs alone cannot reveal what an unchosen action would have achieved. More confident wording is not a value label. |
| Cost and avoided duplicate work | Deduplicated provider request/usage receipts reconciled to the provider total; count retries, failed/unknown charges, construction, refresh and downstream correction. Compare matched workloads and actual frequencies. | Missing receipts cannot be treated as zero; provider invoice scope may exceed application traces. Historical price estimates are not today's verified invoice. |

## Temporal evaluation and future ground truth

1. **Define the target first.** Examples: probability of a specified disclosure within 63 sessions; next-quarter revenue growth using the release available on that date; forward net excess return after the first executable entry. Fix entity, units, horizon, resolution source and revisions policy. Distinguish a new-discovery target from a price forecast.
2. **Freeze the information set.** Each case needs query time, original source version, published time and actual available/ingested time, vintage, memory state and market universe. Enforce cutoff in tools, joins, derived features and memory. A later-revised page carrying an old publication date is not automatically valid historical evidence.
3. **Keep labels out of the agent.** Features and tools cannot access the outcome namespace. The existing stock labels use next observed session open and later observed-session closes at 1/5/21/63/126/252-session horizons; confirm that convention fits the decision before using it. Handle missing/delisted outcomes explicitly.
4. **Use rolling chronological splits.** Train on past windows, select on later validation, then lock a later evaluation window. Purge training examples whose label intervals cross the validation/test boundary; impose any additional embargo for overlap/dependence appropriate to the task. Split related event/issuer/near-duplicate examples together for a separate generalization assessment. Cluster uncertainty by time/event rather than treating millions of overlapping labels as independent.
5. **Respect inspected data.** The older 2018–2021 train / 2022–2023 development / 2024–October 2026 evaluation proposal has already been discussed and inspected during development. Do not declare the whole latter period untouched. Record actual exposure, lock a genuinely unseen selection, and retain a prospective cohort after the current design is frozen. Only use matured labels available by each training cutoff.
6. **Measure the right outcome.** For probabilities use Brier/log scores, calibration and discrimination against base-rate/simple forecasting baselines. For Trading use net excess return, drawdown/risk, turnover and realistic costs/entry availability, alongside the quality of the analysis. For discovery include verified-event yield and lead time at equal capacity, not only P&L.
7. **Address model knowledge.** As-of retrieval does not remove future facts in pretrained weights. Check historical versus living/prospective results, genuinely later outcomes and synthetic controlled worlds where useful. Label historical backtests as historically tool-restricted rather than proven free of model contamination.
8. **Keep distinct conclusions.** Future outcomes can evaluate forecasting and some operationalized research utility. They cannot decide source faithfulness, logical validity, past causal attribution, subjective clarity or the unseen value of every unchosen investigation. Those retain their separate labels above.

Do not collapse everything into one scalar reward initially. Keep factual/derivation errors, useful discovery, reviewer harm, completion, latency and cost visible so optimization cannot buy a high average by suppressing uncertain but valuable investigations.
