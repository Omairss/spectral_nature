Headline

Chip-equipment, optical, and memory shares rose sharply on Nov. 3 after a month-long slide, but the evidence points to several drivers rather than one common cause.

Summary

The rebound was broad but uneven and left most groups far below their earlier levels, so it is a partial recovery rather than a proved turn.

What changed

Chip packaging, test, and factory-equipment suppliers gained about 6.5% on the day, and optical suppliers about 5.1%. The group containing Nvidia and large chipmakers rose only about 3.1%, and memory and hard-disk shares about 3.0%. These groups were still down between about 18% and 37% over the prior 20 sessions.

Why

No single company or policy event explains all groups. Memory pricing is one shared thread: Apple said higher memory costs would lower its hardware profit share, and Micron coverage said more than three-quarters of Micron's 2027 output was already committed. Lattice, Fabrinet, and Lam Research gave separate positive company news. Job openings and factory orders came in above forecasts, and the 10-year government bond rate rose to 4.95%, but those were not the main driver. AMD's pre-report gain reflected its own pending results, not a broad cause.

Context

Memory costs matter because they raise the direct cost of devices and data-center hardware; a durable memory shortage would support memory makers while squeezing buyers such as Apple.

What follows

AMD's after-close results and guidance will show whether the chip recovery extends beyond anticipation; holding gains through more full sessions and fresh memory or equipment order confirmations would strengthen the signal.

Headline

Nvidia Passes Apple as World's Most Valuable Company After Memory-Cost Warning

Summary

Apple warned that rising memory-chip costs will narrow its December-quarter profit margin, pushing its stock down, while chip stocks rebounded and Nvidia's stock-market value moved above Apple's.

What changed

The Nasdaq 100 rose 0.7% to 28,184 on Tuesday as chipmakers rebounded, and Nvidia became the world's most valuable company. Apple had said its December-quarter gross margin, the share of sales left after direct product costs, would be 45% to 46%, below the 46.3% forecast, because memory chips cost more. Apple shares fell 2.2% to $330.97, removing about $107 billion of stock-market value. Nvidia shares rose 2.9% to $207.95, ending a four-session losing streak and adding about $142 billion. At midday, Nvidia was worth $5.01 trillion and Apple was worth $4.83 trillion, leaving Nvidia about $181 billion ahead. Apple had ended Monday about $68 billion ahead.

Why

Higher memory-chip prices raise Apple's costs per device, so investors sold Apple shares. The same price increases benefit chip suppliers, and chip stocks rebounded. Nvidia, a large chip company, gained with the group after four down sessions. Apple's drop and Nvidia's rise together moved Nvidia's stock-market value above Apple's.

Related businesses

The rebound went beyond Nvidia: the VanEck Semiconductor ETF gained 2.3%, and the Technology Select Sector SPDR Fund rose 1.3%.

Brief

Apple warned that more expensive memory chips will cut its December-quarter profit margin, and the stock fell 2.2%, while chip stocks rallied and Nvidia rose 2.9%. At midday, Nvidia became the world's most valuable listed company with a stock-market value of $5.01 trillion, about $181 billion above Apple's $4.83 trillion. The Nasdaq 100 rose 0.7% on the semiconductor rebound, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.4% as Apple declined. The move shows that higher memory prices transfer profit from device makers to chip suppliers.
