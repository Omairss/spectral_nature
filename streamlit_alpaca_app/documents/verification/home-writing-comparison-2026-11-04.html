Selective premarket reaction to U.S. election: broad futures rise while SpaceX, Tesla and defense stocks fall on budget and Texas data-center risk

Investors raised broad market futures but sold policy-linked stocks after the midterms, while the Texas governor result added a specific risk to AI data-center building in a state expected to host 25% of new U.S. capacity.

What changed

On 2026-11-04, before the U.S. open, stock-index futures rose: Dow contracts 1.6%, Russell 2000 contracts 1.3%, S&P 500 contracts nearly 0.9% and Nasdaq 100 contracts 0.3%. SpaceX fell 5.8% to $133.06 and Tesla fell 5.7% to $375.65, erasing about $200 billion in combined stock-market value by 7:30 a.m. ET. Defense contractors also dropped: Lockheed Martin 4%, Northrop Grumman 3.5%, RTX 3.3% and L3Harris 2.9%; Palantir fell 3.7%. Jim Bianco called Gina Hinojosa's Texas governor win the biggest election news for markets, noting Texas is expected to host 25% of data centers coming online between now and 2030.

Why

Broad futures and bonds rose together because a Democratic Congress is expected to slow federal spending growth, easing deficit worries; the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.91% and the dollar weakened. Defense and Palantir shares fell because Democrats are expected to chair congressional committees that write future federal budgets, including the Pentagon's, so investors expect slower spending growth than before; no contract or current revenue has been cut. The Texas result matters to SpaceX and Tesla because both companies are based in Texas and have large AI computing plans. Hinojosa has backed a pause or restrictions on new data-center building, and an earlier state pause was already in place; investors worry it may stay. Brad Gerstner warned that 10-15 gigawatts of the 43 gigawatts of new data-center capacity expected in 2027 might be delayed. But Republicans still control both Texas legislative chambers, so no state rule change is immediate.

Related businesses

The policy-linked sell-off spread beyond defense: prison operators GEO Group fell 6.2%, CoreCivic fell 5.2%, and Trump Media fell 10.6%.

Context

Index futures are bets on where the market will open, so premarket moves show investors adjusting to news before regular trading. Texas's share of planned U.S. data-center capacity means local building approvals directly affect AI companies' expansion.

What follows

Watch whether Texas's existing data-center building pause is lifted or extended after the new governor and legislature take office; a clear decision would confirm or reduce the AI-building delay risk.

The U.S. midterm election split early trading on November 4, 2026. Headline

Nvidia becomes the world's most valuable company as chip shares rebound

Summary

Nvidia's market value rose about $142 billion and Apple's fell about $107 billion after Apple said memory costs would squeeze its profit rate.

What changed

On November 3, AI-hardware and semiconductor shares recovered broadly after a month-long slide, but the gains were uneven. The chip packaging, testing and factory-equipment supplier group rose about 6.5%, and optical suppliers about 5.1%. The group containing Nvidia and chip-manufacturing companies rose about 3.1%, while memory and disk-drive shares rose about 3.0%. Nvidia rose 2.9%, adding about $142 billion in market value and becoming the world's most valuable company. Apple fell 2.2%, losing about $107 billion, after it said its December-quarter hardware profit rate would be 45% to 46%, below the 46.3% analysts expected, because memory costs rose. SanDisk gained about 5.5% and Micron more than 3% in intraday reports.

Why

Rising memory prices were the clearest shared driver. Apple's lower profit forecast showed those costs hitting hardware makers, and its CEO said memory prices would keep rising beyond the holiday quarter. The same tight supply helped memory and storage stocks; more than three-quarters of Micron's 2027 output was already committed. Company-specific reports added separate support: Lattice Semiconductor rose about 14% after record quarterly sales of $291 million, up 118% from a year earlier; Fabrinet rose about 13.7% after sales rose 51% to $1.47 billion and its current-quarter outlook beat forecasts; and Lam Research's shareholder meeting forecast factory-equipment spending above $200 billion in 2027. Stronger job openings and factory orders supported the broader market, but they were not the main driver. No single cause accounts for all groups.

Context

The rebound followed a long decline. Over the previous 20 sessions, the groups that rose on November 3 were still down between roughly 18% and 37%, so the gains were a partial recovery, not a return to earlier prices.

What follows

Watch whether the rebound holds in subsequent sessions and whether new memory-price or factory-equipment order reports confirm the supply-tightness explanation.

Brief

The November 3 chip rebound was broad but uneven: chip packaging, testing and factory-equipment suppliers gained about 6.5%, optical suppliers about 5.1%, while the group with Nvidia and chip-manufacturing companies gained about 3.1% and memory and disk-drive shares about 3.0%. These moves recovered only part of a prior 20-session decline of roughly 18% to 37%. The clearest shared driver was memory pricing: Apple's lower profit guidance because of memory costs pushed Apple down and supported memory-related stocks, while Nvidia's rise made it the world's most valuable company. Company-specific results lifted Lattice and Fabrinet, and Lam Research's 2027 equipment-spending outlook supported factory-equipment suppliers. The main open question is whether the rebound continues when fresh memory or equipment data arrive and whether the gains hold.
